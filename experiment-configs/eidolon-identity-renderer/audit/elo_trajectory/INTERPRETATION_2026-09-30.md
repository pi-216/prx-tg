# How to read the ELO ranking — and what it does NOT say

**Date:** 2026-09-30 · Companion to `RESULTS.md` and `summary.json` in this directory
(generated by `scripts/harness/elo_results.py` — do not edit those, edit this).

## What the instrument is

* **Ordinal, pool-local, relative.** It orders renders *within one fixed pool*. It is not a
  quality score on any absolute scale and cannot answer "how far from good".
* Every pair in these pools is the **same identity at two checkpoints**, so the order is about
  render quality, not identity preference.
* **Resolution limit:** the field spans only ~300–340 Elo with RD ≈ 130, and per-pool SNR is
  1.14–1.26 against the tool's 5.0 "labels good enough" line. **A single 10-image session
  cannot resolve a difference smaller than ≈2·RD ≈ 270 Elo.** Replication (more identity pools
  + a pre-registered split test) is the design that buys resolution.

## What it does NOT say — correcting the first framing

The reported 6000–10000 tie is an **instrument resolution limit**, not evidence that quality
stopped improving. The earlier framing ("the run had given up what it was going to give by
~6000") was an **overclaim**. The supported statement is: *differences among 6000–10000 are
below this session's resolution*. **Absence of resolvable difference ≠ absence of difference.**

## Real-photo anchors: REJECTED (operator, 2026-09-30)

Anchors would win by construction — the operator can state that without a vote — so they add no
information, and worse, they **re-introduce the resemblance-to-real criterion this project
retired** with the recogniser gate. Reproduction is not a goal; the criterion is render quality.
For the record the sources do exist in principle (the validation loader draws samples from the
stratum tree), but the exact `recon_<idx>` → source mapping has not been reconstructed, and it
is not worth reconstructing: **do not add real photos to a quality pool.**

## Operator hypothesis (2026-09-30) — and its two separable halves

> "There was still a lot of room to gain render quality, but the learning rate started dropping,
> and the model didn't have the power to make big shifts."

**(a) SCHEDULE half — SUPPORTED by the run's own log.** Peak lr 3.0e-4 → floor 1e-6, a **300×
collapse**, of which **33× happens in the last 2,000 steps**; the final 1,000 steps ran at
1e-6 while the loss stalled and ticked up (1.406 @9000 → 1.4253 @10000). A plateau at the tail
of a cosine-to-floor schedule is **expected by construction** — the last fifth of the run was
asked to refine with ~1/300th of the initial step size.

**(b) POWER half — plausible, with a concrete mechanism.** The spatial token grid is coarse:
patch 2 over a 128² latent = **64×64 tokens = 16 px per token** at 1024² output. Teeth,
hairline and ears are high-frequency structure at exactly the scale that grid cannot address,
and parameter count (235M) does not repair a coarse grid.

**They predict different fixes**, which is what makes the next arm decidable: (a) → a *fresh
cosine* continuation (the arm's §9.3 cheap schedule probe); (b) → *finer latent/token
resolution* or a different output space. One experiment separates them.

## The one absolute signal we have

**Detection attrition:** 34/160 renders (21%) admit no detectable face+neck at all under
Sapiens2 5B seg2, versus **0/150 real faces**. Crude but absolute and model-independent, and it
agrees with the operator's read that these renders are "barely recognizable as faces at all".
It is **not** a ranking proxy (Spearman +0.50 against the human ranking, driven entirely by
step 1000).
