# How to read the ELO ranking — and what it does NOT say

**Date:** 2026-09-30 · Companion to `RESULTS.md` and `summary.json` in this directory
(generated by `scripts/harness/elo_results.py` — do not edit those, edit this).

## What the instrument is

* **Ordinal, pool-local, relative.** It orders renders *within one fixed pool*. It is not a
  quality score on any absolute scale and cannot answer "how far from good".
* Every pair in these pools is the **same identity at two checkpoints**, so the order is about
  render quality, not identity preference.
* **Resolution limit:** the field spans only ~300–340 Elo with RD ≈ 130, and per-pool SNR is
  1.14–1.26 against the tool's 5.0 "labels good enough" line. **A single 10-image session
  cannot resolve a difference smaller than ≈2·RD ≈ 270 Elo.** Replication (more identity pools
  + a pre-registered split test) is the design that buys resolution.

## What it does NOT say — correcting the first framing

The reported 6000–10000 tie is an **instrument resolution limit**, not evidence that quality
stopped improving. The earlier framing ("the run had given up what it was going to give by
~6000") was an **overclaim**. The supported statement is: *differences among 6000–10000 are
below this session's resolution*. **Absence of resolvable difference ≠ absence of difference.**

## Real-photo anchors: REJECTED (operator, 2026-09-30)

Anchors would win by construction — the operator can state that without a vote — so they add no
information, and worse, they **re-introduce the resemblance-to-real criterion this project
retired** with the recogniser gate. Reproduction is not a goal; the criterion is render quality.
For the record the sources do exist in principle (the validation loader draws samples from the
stratum tree), but the exact `recon_<idx>` → source mapping has not been reconstructed, and it
is not worth reconstructing: **do not add real photos to a quality pool.**

## Operator hypothesis (2026-09-30) — what is testable in it

> "There was still a lot of room to gain render quality, but the learning rate started dropping,
> and the model didn't have the power to make big shifts."

**(a) SCHEDULE half — SUPPORTED by the run's own log.** Peak lr 3.0e-4 → floor 1e-6, a **300×
collapse**, of which **33× happens in the last 2,000 steps**; the final 1,000 steps ran at
1e-6 while the loss stalled and ticked up (1.406 @9000 → 1.4253 @10000). A plateau at the tail
of a cosine-to-floor schedule is **expected by construction** — the last fifth of the run was
asked to refine with ~1/300th of the initial step size.

**(b) The second half of the hypothesis is NOT nameable yet — and is not named.** The sentence
"the model didn't have the power to make big shifts" is quoted above and left **undefined on
purpose**. "Power" is not a term this project uses: it appears in no ledger, provenance, README,
tree, plan or AGENTS.md (checked 2026-09-30); the only occurrences in the repo are in *this file*,
because this file quotes it. An earlier draft of this note turned it into a "(b) POWER half —
with a concrete mechanism", which promoted a loose word into a hypothesis by assumption. Struck.

Three **distinct** readings, each implying a different experiment, are worth separating — but the
operator must say which was meant:

* **capacity** — too few parameters/depth to represent the structure → widen or deepen.
* **spatial resolution** — the token grid is too coarse to address it: patch 2 over a 128² latent
  is a **64×64 grid = 16 px per token** at 1024² output, and teeth / hairline / ears are
  high-frequency structure at exactly that scale. Fix: finer grid (patch 1) or higher latent res.
* **optimization mobility** — the model could represent it, but training never *travels* there;
  update sizes / gradient signal too small to reach that region, independent of the LR floor.
  Fix: schedule, objective, or init.

The fresh-cosine continuation tests the **schedule half only**. A flat result there does not
choose among these three, and must not be reported as if it did.

**What is decidable today:** the **schedule half only**, by one experiment — a fresh-cosine
continuation. The other half is not decidable yet because it has not been named; naming it comes
*after* the schedule half is answered, since a schedule-limited plateau is the cheaper and
prior explanation and would make the other one moot for now.

## Measurement design — density, not spacing (operator correction, 2026-09-30)

An earlier recommendation in this session was to sample the Elo pool at ≥2k spacing because
"adjacent checkpoints are below the ~270 Elo resolution". **That recommendation was wrong**, and
the operator's objection ("my evaluation is not a perfect sort … offering more images, even if
neighbours don't differ much, will give you a stronger overall signal") is right, for two reasons:

1. **Wrong resolution applied.** The ~270 Elo figure is the resolution of a *single pairwise
   claim* (z = Δelo / √(RD₁²+RD₂²)). The estimand here is an **aggregate ordering** over the whole
   set, whose sensitivity improves with the *number of comparisons and items* — each item's rating
   is anchored by many opponents, and random judgment error averages out. Non-transitivity in
   judgments ("A>B and B>C but C>A") is not an anomaly to avoid; it is what an Elo system exists
   to absorb, and a strict sort would break on it. Trading a little per-comparison information for
   many more comparisons is the right trade for a trajectory.
2. **The tool agrees with the operator.** `elo-picker/app/commands/pick_comparison.rb` phase 3:
   *"once everyone is trusted, refinement is about resolving adjacency: pair the closest-rated
   uncompared images (min |Δelo|) to drive the scores apart."* After trust, the tool deliberately
   spends clicks on the **most similar** pairs. Skipping neighbours discards exactly the clicks it
   considers most valuable.

**The cost is clicks, and it is quadratic.** The tool is exhaustive by design — *"every pair is
judged exactly once; when every pair is done the run is over"* — so a pool of n costs C(n,2)
comparisons. Observed: all six 10-item pools are exactly 45 = C(10,2) with 9 games per image.

| pool size | comparisons | split-test null p (half vs half) |
|---|---|---|
| n=11 (1k grid) | 55 | 1/C(11,5) = 2.2e-3 |
| n=21 (500 grid) | 210 | 1/C(21,11) = 2.8e-6 |
| n=41 (500 grid, 5k→20k) | 820 | 1/C(41,20) — impractical |

**Structural limit worth stating once:** the tool's own `SNR ≥ 5` gate is **unreachable** for a
within-run field ~300 Elo wide. SNR = spread/(2·avg RD) would need avg RD ≤ 30, i.e. ~130 games
per image, against a maximum of n−1 games. Dense pools therefore do **not** exist to raise SNR —
they exist to sharpen the *aggregate* ordering (split test, Spearman, per-checkpoint mean rank).
**Gate on the aggregate statistics, never on SNR.**

Consequence for this arm: the 500-step grid serves the Elo pools as well as the automatic
trajectory, and no separate sampling rule is needed — which also removes the HARKing risk a
post-hoc spacing choice would have carried.

## The one absolute signal we have

**Detection attrition:** 34/160 renders (21%) admit no detectable face+neck at all under
Sapiens2 5B seg2, versus **0/150 real faces**. Crude but absolute and model-independent, and it
agrees with the operator's read that these renders are "barely recognizable as faces at all".
It is **not** a ranking proxy (Spearman +0.50 against the human ranking, driven entirely by
step 1000).
